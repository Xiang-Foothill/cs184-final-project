StyleSlider

Controllable Geometric Stylization of 3D Meshes

Diffusion-guided 3D stylization can exaggerate a mesh’s proportions, elongate its features, and make it look cartoon-like, but it gives little control over how much the shape changes. StyleSlider is an interactive system that lets a user dial geometric stylization continuously, from the original mesh at α = 0 to a fully stylized target at α = 1, by interpolating vertex deformation fields or cage control points. We will study how to keep the in-between shapes smooth, predictable, and geometrically valid.

Problem description

Geometric stylization changes the actual shape of a 3D object. Traditional style transfer mostly changes colors and textures; geometric stylization changes silhouettes and proportions. It matters for animation, character design, digital sculpting, and artistic 3D modeling.

Recent methods such as GeoStyle [1] show that diffusion models can guide large geometric changes while keeping an object’s semantic identity. A character’s limbs can become elongated, its head exaggerated, or its silhouette reshaped to match a reference artistic style.

These methods run an optimization that produces one stylized result. They give no direct, predictable control over how much the shape is exaggerated, and a user may want a moderate transformation instead of the strongest output. Re-running the optimization with different parameters is expensive and can give inconsistent results.

Research question

Can we create a continuous and intuitive control over geometric stylization strength while maintaining smooth and valid mesh deformation?

1.1Approach: one strength parameter

We add an explicit stylization parameter α ∈ [0, 1]. Instead of running a diffusion model again for every strength, we interpolate between the original mesh and its stylized deformation.

α = 0Original mesh
α = 0.5Intermediate stylization
α = 1Fully stylized target

For an original mesh with vertices V0 and a stylized mesh V1 with the same topology, the basic interpolation moves every vertex along its own displacement:

V(α) = V0 + α (V1 − V0) (1)

We will also interpolate the control points of a coarse deformation cage, then pass the motion to the enclosed mesh through cage weights wj computed once on the original mesh. A cage is a lower-dimensional representation, so it may give smoother and more coherent shape changes than moving every vertex directly.

c(α) = c0 + α (c1 − c0),    v(α) = ∑j wj cj(α) (2)

1.2Why this is hard

Interpolation does not guarantee good geometry. Intermediate meshes can show the failures below, and an equal step in α may not look like an equal step in exaggeration. We will measure how stylization strength relates to geometric quality and to how exaggerated the shape looks.

  • Unwanted stretchingEdges grow far past their rest length.
  • Self-intersectionsParts of the surface pass through each other.
  • Collapsed trianglesFaces become degenerate or flip.
  • Uneven deformationSome regions change abruptly while others barely move.

The project focuses on geometric deformation and interactive graphics. We are not training a new generative model. We start from precomputed stylized meshes, including compatible examples from existing methods, so the core project does not depend on reproducing a diffusion pipeline.

1.3System pipeline

Mesh processing

Load original and stylized meshes, verify vertex correspondence, compute vertex displacements, and build deformation cages where needed.

Deformation algorithms

Vertex interpolation as the main baseline; cage interpolation as the second method, with precomputed weights that carry control-point motion to the mesh.

Interactive rendering

A viewer that updates vertex positions as the user drags the strength slider. Existing libraries handle file loading, rendering, and geometric analysis.

Goals and deliverables

Minimum successful outcome

A stable slider-based system with direct vertex interpolation, a working cage-based comparison, and documented quality and performance measurements. Advanced interpolation and perceptual control come after that, if time permits.

2.1What we plan to deliver

  1. G1

    Controllable mesh stylization

    A deformation pipeline that takes an original mesh and a matching stylized target and interpolates between them with a user-set α. We start with linear interpolation of vertex displacement fields. Both meshes share topology and vertex correspondence, so every vertex can be tracked through the deformation. We test on at least three objects with different geometric character, such as an animal, a humanoid character, and a simple decorative object.

    OutputFor each object, renders at five strengths: 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%.
  2. G2

    Cage-based deformation control

    A coarse cage around the original mesh. We interpolate the cage control points and propagate the motion to the enclosed mesh, then compare against vertex interpolation for deformation smoothness, geometric distortion, and computational performance.

    OutputSide-by-side comparisons of the two methods, showing differences in silhouette, mesh deformation, and geometric quality.
  3. G3

    Interactive 3D visualization

    A demo where users load a mesh and adjust its stylization strength with a slider, fast enough for smooth interactive use. The interface provides:

    • A slider for stylization strength from 0% to 100%
    • A 3D viewer with camera rotation and zoom
    • Real-time updates to the deformed mesh
    • Switching between several example meshes
    • A comparison between the original and the current stylized geometry
    OutputA live demonstration and a short video of the exaggeration changing continuously as the slider moves.
  4. G4

    Evaluation of geometric quality and controllability

    Quantitative and qualitative tests of whether a single strength parameter gives meaningful control. We measure the four metrics in 2.4 and plot each one as a function of α.

    OutputDisplacement-versus-strength plots, geometric distortion comparisons, and performance measurements.

2.2What we hope to deliver

  1. S1

    Geometry-aware interpolation

    Linear interpolation is cheap but can produce poor intermediate shapes. We will try Laplacian smoothing and As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) regularization, and compare them with linear interpolation to see whether the extra constraints improve the in-between results.

  2. S2

    Perceptually uniform strength control

    A linear α does not give a linear increase in perceived stylization. Growing a character’s head from 100% to 120% can be much less noticeable than growing it from 140% to 160%. We will test nonlinear mappings from slider value to deformation strength and, if feasible, run a small user study in which participants rate the perceived strength of intermediate shapes.

  3. S3

    Localized strength control

    Different strengths in different regions of one mesh: exaggerate a character’s head while keeping its body nearly unchanged, or elongate an animal’s legs without changing its torso. This means blending local deformation fields while keeping the transitions between edited and unedited regions smooth.

2.3Images and demo we will produce

Our main visual result shows the same object at five strengths, rendered from the same camera viewpoints and lighting so the changes can be compared directly. We will also show vertex and cage interpolation side by side, with wireframe overlays that reveal stretching and distortion.

2.4How we will measure quality and performance

MetricWhat we measure at each strength
Geometric validityNumber of degenerate triangles, inverted or flipped faces, and detected self-intersections.
Deformation smoothnessChanges in neighboring vertex displacements and local edge-length distortion, to find abrupt or uneven changes.
Monotonic strengthDisplacement from the original mesh: does a larger slider value always mean a larger geometric change? We keep this separate from perceived exaggeration, which may not be linear.
PerformanceTime to update the mesh at different strengths, for vertex-based and cage-based interpolation.

We plot each metric against α. All comparisons use fixed original–target mesh pairs, so the methods are evaluated under identical conditions.

2.5Questions we will answer

  1. Q1Controllability

    Does interpolation give a smooth and predictable progression from the original mesh to the stylized target?

  2. Q2Geometric quality

    Which interpolation strategy better preserves mesh quality, especially for large exaggerations?

  3. Q3Efficiency

    Can stylization strength be adjusted interactively, without re-running expensive generative optimization?

Resources

4.1Related research

  1. [1]

    Choi et al. Image-Guided Geometric Stylization of 3D Meshes. CVPR 2026

    Image-guided geometric stylization with diffusion guidance and a coarse-to-fine deformation pipeline. This is our main motivation and our reference for cage-based geometric stylization. We focus on continuous strength control over the resulting deformation and do not reproduce its full generative pipeline.

  2. [2]

    Dinh et al. Geometry in Style: 3D Stylization via Surface Normal Deformation. CVPR 2025

    Surface-normal-based deformation with differentiable ARAP regularization, for expressive stylization that keeps object identity. It motivates our study of geometric quality and possible regularization of intermediate shapes.

  3. [3]

    Wynn et al. Morpheus: Text-Driven 3D Gaussian Splat Shape and Color Stylization. CVPR 2025

    Adds control over appearance and shape stylization strength for 3D Gaussian Splatting, which makes it the closest prior work on strength control. Our project works with explicit triangle meshes and analyzes the geometry of the intermediate meshes.

4.3Platform and starting point

Development and baseline interpolation run on standard desktop hardware. We may use a GPU for advanced experiments, but the core project does not need GPU-based diffusion model training.

Existing libraries handle file loading, rendering, and geometric analysis. We will refer to existing implementations and public mesh assets where they are compatible, such as the examples on the GeoStyle project page [1], and write our own interpolation, cage deformation, and evaluation code.
